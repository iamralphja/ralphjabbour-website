About

I study why people and organizations refuse to act on what they already know.

Ralph Jabbour is a Dubai-based decision strategist, business analyst, and author. His perspective was shaped over fifteen years inside commercial and operational systems, where he learned how incentives, identities, and cultural patterns shape the decisions people make and delay.

His work applies the same diagnostic thinking to people and organizations: identifying inherited patterns, exposing systems that have outlived their purpose, and confronting the decisions being delayed. People and organizations rarely remain stuck because they lack information. They remain stuck because acting threatens identity, incentives, or belonging. Awareness without action becomes another form of avoidance. The Rebellion Project is the body of work built around that tension. The Installed Life names the diagnosis. Decision Ownership defines the response. He is the author of The Rebellion of Life: Burn the Lie and the host of Letters from Rebellion.

What he offers is not a roadmap. It is a mirror, and the courage to look into it.

The Rebellion Project is the ongoing body of work that names this, exposes it, and refuses to make it comfortable.

Speaking

Organizations do not resist change. They protect what change would threaten.

Ralph’s keynotes examine why capable leaders can describe a problem clearly while their organizations continue protecting the conditions that produce it. He connects human behavior with organizational systems to show how identity, incentives, and belonging shape what leaders are willing to confront.

Each talk gives the audience a language for examining what the current system rewards, what it protects, and which decisions it keeps postponing. The value lies in a sharper standard for judgment that remains useful after the event ends.

His perspective is grounded in fifteen years inside commercial and operational systems. He connects questions of identity, responsibility, and courage to the decisions leaders face at work, where competing incentives and real consequences test every principle.

Keynote Topics

The Cost of Knowing: Why Clarity Still Fails to Produce Action

TThe flagship keynote on the gap between recognition and action. It examines how intelligent people and well-informed organizations use analysis, consensus, and preparation to postpone decisions—and what Decision Ownership requires once the problem is already clear.

The Installed Culture: Why Organizations Protect What No Longer Works

This keynote examines how repeated decisions, rewards, and tolerated behaviors become an operating culture no one consciously chose. It gives leaders a way to identify what the system protects, why people keep reproducing it, and which decision could interrupt the pattern.

Decision Ownership: Acting Without the Comfort of Certainty

A keynote on making consequential choices when evidence is incomplete, outcomes remain uncertain, and every available path carries a cost. It separates responsibility from certainty and examines why postponing a decision is still a decision—with consequences that compound.

The Book

The Rebellion of Life

Burn the Lie

Published 2025
Format Paperback · Digital
ISBN 9798293242559

The Rebellion of Life begins with an accusation: the life you resent may be the life you kept choosing. The life that doesn't fit you, didn't happen by accident. It was built one small surrender at a time. It is a manifesto for people who have outgrown the identities, ambitions, and beliefs that once made their lives feel stable.

Across seventeen chapters, the book examines truth, cowardice, indecision, creativity, discipline, suffering, freedom, and purpose. Its central argument is simple: an installed life is built through inherited assumptions and repeated compromises, then preserved by every decision postponed. It must be confronted the same way it was built one choice at a time.

From truth to cowardice, indecision to creativity, collapse to what comes after The book offers no promise of comfort. It asks the reader to examine what they already know, what they continue to protect, and what another year of avoidance will cost.

Not for a better life. A truer one.
